📜 This Day in History

1998 — San Jose adopts its Living Wage Policy. On November 17, 1998, the City Council adopted Resolution 68554, establishing the city’s Living Wage Policy for workers on city service contracts and jobs backed by city financial assistance — a policy still on the books today.

2003 — the Terminator becomes the Governator. On November 17, 2003, Arnold Schwarzenegger was sworn in in Sacramento as California’s 38th governor, after winning the October recall election that removed Gov. Gray Davis; his first act rescinded the vehicle-license-fee increase.

1968 — the Heidi Game, in Oakland. On November 17, 1968, the Oakland Raiders scored two touchdowns in the final minute to beat the New York Jets 43–32 at the Oakland Coliseum — but East Coast viewers never saw it: NBC had cut away to the film Heidi. The uproar changed how American networks broadcast sports.

1947 — the transistor stirs (tech heritage, from New Jersey). On November 17, 1947, John Bardeen and Walter Brattain at Bell Labs began the month of experiments in which they observed the basic principles of the transistor — the device that, a decade later, made this valley’s industry possible. It happened in New Jersey; we claim the inheritance, not the address.

1777 — the Articles of Confederation go to the states. The young republic’s first frame of government was submitted to the states for ratification on November 17, 1777. A nearer anniversary falls later this month: San Jose itself was founded on November 29, 1777 — not today, whatever the fun-facts pages say.

1869 — the Suez Canal opens. The canal linking the Mediterranean with the Red Sea was inaugurated in Egypt on November 17, 1869, redrawing the world’s trade routes in a day.

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Tuesday, November 17 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up — go early, before the clouds close in

First Quarter Moon today — and tonight’s sky is on a timer. The Moon reached its exact first quarter at 3:48 AM PT this morning, about half lit, and tonight it rides high in the south at sundown and sets around 12:19 AM. Tonight starts partly cloudy and turns mostly cloudy, with rain likely after 2:00 AM — so the viewing window is the early evening, in the gaps, not the small hours.

Evening, for everyone: Saturn, early. Saturn is the lone bright evening planet, golden and steady in the southeast, highest at about 8:53 PM at magnitude about 0.5–0.6 — bright enough to punch through valley light and thin cloud alike. Around it: the Great Square of Pegasus overhead, Cassiopeia in the northeast, the Pleiades climbing in the east, and the half-lit Moon high in the south. Catch it before the cloud deck thickens.

The Leonids peak tonight — honestly, the valley will mostly miss them. The shower’s predicted peak falls at 4:00 PM PT today, and its best hours are after midnight, once Leo is up and the Moon has set. But the same forecast that brings Wednesday’s rain has tonight mostly cloudy with rain likely after 2:00 AM, and Wednesday night is wetter still. Under dark skies the Leonids would run 10–15 an hour; from the valley tonight, expect at best a stray bright meteor through a cloud gap in the early evening. The Mt. Hamilton drive, our usual upgrade, cannot beat a storm deck — save it for a clear night.

Before dawn, if the clouds break: the Leo pair. Jupiter blazes at magnitude −2.1 and Mars rides at about 0.7, still only about 1.2° apart in Leo after their Nov 15 closest pass — the same patch of sky the Leonids radiate from. Venus dazzles at about −4.6, rising at 4:12 AM low in the east. All three are forecast-weather permitting tonight.
