📜 This Day in History

Monday, November 2

Today itself — Día de los Muertos, the day for remembering adults. November 2 is the second and principal day of Día de los Muertos (November 1 remembers children), and San Jose marks it today with community ofrendas open all day at the public library’s Bascom Branch and Biblioteca Latinoamericana — the valley’s living tradition, happening on this date, in this city. The honest local note: our check of the local timelines found no San Jose founding-era event dated November 2, so today’s box leads with the observance itself and goes wider below, labelled as such.

1947 — California’s Spruce Goose flies, once. On November 2 in Long Beach Harbor, Howard Hughes lifted the wooden H-4 Hercules — wingspan 320 feet, then the largest airplane ever flown — about 70 feet off the water for roughly a mile on what was billed as a taxi test. It never flew again, but the one minute answered his critics in full.

1920 — Commercial radio is born, reading election returns. Pittsburgh’s KDKA made the first licensed commercial broadcast on November 2, announcing the Harding–Cox presidential results — a fitting entry on the eve of our own Election Day, when the returns will arrive by rather faster means.

1889 — Two Dakotas join the Union. President Benjamin Harrison signed the proclamations admitting North Dakota and South Dakota as the 39th and 40th states on November 2 — shuffling the papers first, so no one could say which was signed first.

1983 — Martin Luther King Jr. Day is signed into law. President Ronald Reagan signed the bill creating the federal holiday on November 2; it was first observed as a federal holiday in 1986 and is now marked on the third Monday of January.

2000 — The first resident crew reaches the space station. On November 2, Expedition 1 docked with the International Space Station, beginning the continuous human presence in orbit that has now run for 26 years — the valley’s tech age, written in the sky.

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Monday, November 2 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up

Saturn owns the evening again — early is better tonight. Saturn is up at dark and rides high in the south through the evening, a steady golden naked-eye point from any valley sidewalk; its rings want a small telescope. The catch is the forecast: the NWS calls tonight mostly cloudy, so the best window is early evening, before the cloud deck thickens ahead of tomorrow’s rain.

No Moon tonight — it is saving itself for the planets. The Moon is a waning crescent a day past last quarter and does not rise until the small hours. When it does, it joins a pre-dawn gathering in Leo: the crescent passes near Mars (closest around 5:40 AM) and Jupiter, with bright Regulus in tow — the Nov 2–3 “moon, Mars and Jupiter” dance that runs both mornings. Set an alarm if the sky clears before sunrise.

The rest of the board. Mars rises around 1:00 AM and Jupiter about 30 minutes later, the pair drawing toward their close meeting on Nov 15–16. Venus brightens low in the southeast before sunrise later this week. Mercury passes inferior conjunction on Nov 4 and is lost in the Sun’s glare — do not hunt for it. The South Taurid meteors peak Nov 4–5: few in number, but slow and bright.

Honest about our sky. The valley floor is bright — from town you will get the planets and the boldest stars, and on a cloudy night, honestly, perhaps only gaps between clouds. For the rest, it is 45 minutes up Mt. Hamilton Road toward Lick Observatory: at 4,200 feet the Milky Way has structure, the Pleiades become a cluster instead of a smudge, and Saturn’s rings in a small scope will stop a conversation.
