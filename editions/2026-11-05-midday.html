📜 This Day in History

Thursday, November 5

An honest local note first. Our check of the San Jose / Santa Clara Valley timelines found no founding-era local event dated November 5, so today’s box goes wider — and says so, as this feature does whenever the local well runs dry for the date. (The local anniversary is coming: San José’s pueblo founding was November 29, 1777.)

1605 — The Gunpowder Plot is foiled in London. In the small hours of November 5, royal guards found Guy Fawkes in a cellar under the House of Lords, guarding barrels of gunpowder meant to blow up King James I and Parliament at the state opening. Britain still marks the night with bonfires and fireworks — Guy Fawkes Night.

1872 — Susan B. Anthony votes — and is arrested for it. In Rochester, New York, suffragist Susan B. Anthony cast a ballot in the presidential election on November 5, nearly half a century before the 19th Amendment. She was arrested, tried, and fined — and refused to pay — in a case that carried the suffrage argument across the country.

1912 — Woodrow Wilson wins a three-way race for the White House. On November 5, Democrat Woodrow Wilson defeated President William Howard Taft and former president Theodore Roosevelt, running as a Progressive — one of the rare elections in which a third-party candidate outpolled an incumbent president.

1935 — Parker Brothers launches Monopoly. The real-estate board game went on sale on November 5, in the middle of the Great Depression — and went on to become one of the best-selling board games in the world, family arguments included.

1940 — Franklin D. Roosevelt wins an unprecedented third term. On November 5, voters returned FDR to the White House for a third term, a first in American history; the 22nd Amendment, ratified in 1951, later limited presidents to two elected terms.

Set each morning; carried forward at midday and evening. Sources: Britannica — Today in History: November 5 · On This Day — November 5

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Thursday, November 5 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up

Saturn is the evening planet — and tonight the sky is forecast mostly clear. Saturn is up at dark and high in the south, a steady golden naked-eye point, with the Great Square of Pegasus overhead and the Pleiades rising in the east. The NWS forecast for tonight is mostly clear, low near 50°F — a better viewing night than last night’s clouds.

Taurids still active, a night past the peak. The Southern Taurid meteors reached their main peak overnight Nov 4–5 and remain active tonight — only about 5–10 an hour even under ideal skies, but slow, bright, and prone to fireballs. The thin waning crescent Moon stays out of the evening sky, so it causes little interference. The radiant, in Taurus near Aldebaran and the Pleiades, rides highest around midnight, which is the best time to watch. The Northern Taurids follow, peaking Nov 11–12.

Before dawn: Venus, Mars and Jupiter — and the Moon joins Venus tomorrow. The morning sky holds brilliant Venus, reddish Mars, and bright Jupiter, all naked-eye. On the mornings of Nov 6–7 the waning crescent Moon passes near Venus and the star Spica before sunrise. Mercury, by contrast, passed inferior conjunction on Nov 4 and is lost in the Sun’s glare; do not go looking for it this week.

Honest about our sky. The valley floor is bright — from town you will get Saturn, the planets and the boldest stars on a clear night like tonight, and a Taurid fireball is exactly the kind of thing that can punch through city light. For the fainter meteors and the rest, it is 45 minutes up Mt. Hamilton Road toward Lick Observatory: at 4,200 feet the Milky Way has structure and Saturn’s rings in a small scope will stop a conversation.
