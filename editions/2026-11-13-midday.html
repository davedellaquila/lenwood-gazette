📜 This Day in History

Friday, November 13

1849 — San José becomes California’s first capital. The San José Public Library’s history timeline dates San José’s tenure as the first capital of the new State of California from November 13, 1849 to May 1, 1851 — the valley town where California’s first state legislature sat, before statehood itself arrived in September 1850 and before the capital wandered on to Vallejo, Sacramento, Benicia, and Sacramento again. The local lead, on the exact date: this is the day the timeline’s first-capital chapter opens.

1942 — The Naval Battle of Guadalcanal’s “Friday the 13th” fight. In the early hours of November 13, 1942, U.S. and Japanese fleets met at point-blank range off Guadalcanal. The heavy cruiser USS San Francisco — flagship in the fight, later memorialized in the city she was named for — was heavily damaged, and both U.S. admirals in the action were killed. The battle helped turn the Guadalcanal campaign.

1940 — Fantasia premieres. Walt Disney’s Fantasia opened as a roadshow at New York’s Broadway Theatre on November 13, 1940 — the animated concert film, made at Disney’s Burbank studio in California, that put classical music and animation on the same screen.

1982 — The Vietnam Veterans Memorial is dedicated. Thousands of veterans marched in Washington, D.C., on November 13, 1982, for the dedication of the Vietnam Veterans Memorial — the black granite wall that changed how America builds its war memorials.

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Friday, November 13 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up

Saturn after the rain — tonight turns partly cloudy, so take the sky in windows. Saturn is again the evening planet — up at dark, high in the south, a steady golden point bright enough to show through thin cloud gaps — with the Great Square of Pegasus overhead, Cassiopeia in the northeast, and the Pleiades climbing in the east. The Moon is a waxing crescent, about 15–16% lit, and sets early; today the Moon is at apogee, its farthest point of this orbit, about 405,600 km away.

Before dawn Saturday: a four-planet alignment opens. From November 13 to 22, Mercury, Venus, Mars, and Jupiter stretch across the eastern morning sky before sunrise. Venus blazes brightest; higher in Leo, Mars and Jupiter are closing toward their close meeting on the mornings around Nov 15–16. Saturday dawns partly cloudy-to-partly sunny, so the pre-dawn hours are worth a look if the clouds part.

The Leonids are building. The Leonids are active now (Nov 6–30) and peak around Nov 17–18, at up to about 15 meteors an hour under ideal dark skies — a gentle shower this year, with no storm expected. Tonight is a build-up night, not the peak: after midnight, under a dark sky, is the better window.

Honest about our sky. From the valley floor tonight you will get Saturn, the boldest stars, and the crescent if the post-rain clouds thin — faint meteors will mostly be lost to city light and passing cloud, and that is the honest trade. The Mt. Hamilton upgrade toward Lick Observatory opens darker sky, but on a wet-roads night after a storm, tonight’s early window from home is the play.
