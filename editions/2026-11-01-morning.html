📜 This Day in History

Sunday, November 1

1868 — San Jose’s first horsecar trip. On November 1, the horse-railroad cars made their initial run from First Street in San Jose to Main Street in Santa Clara — work on the line along the Alameda had begun that August 31, after the Legislature granted the franchise that March. It was the valley’s first street-railway service, a decade before the lines were consolidated. Source below: county history.

1776 — A California mission is dedicated. On November 1, Father Junípero Serra dedicated Mission San Juan Capistrano, the seventh of Alta California’s 21 missions — a year before San Jose’s own pueblo was founded up this same coast. Wider California, honestly labelled: the local well for November 1 is led by the horsecar, not crowded.

1512 — The Sistine Chapel ceiling is shown. Michelangelo’s ceiling was exhibited to the public for the first time on November 1 — the wider world’s entry today, included because it is the date’s most durable mark.

1952 — The first thermonuclear test. The United States detonated “Ivy Mike” at Enewetak Atoll on November 1, the first full-scale thermonuclear device — a sobering November 1 that reshaped the century the valley’s tech age would inherit.

Set each morning; carried forward at midday and evening. Sources: History of Santa Clara County (1922) — Part 29 · This Week in California History — Nov 1 · Britannica — On This Day, November 1

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Sunday, November 1 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up

The evening belongs to Saturn. Well past opposition, Saturn is already up at dark and rides high in the south through the evening — a steady, golden, naked-eye point from any sidewalk in the valley; the rings themselves want a small telescope. It crosses the sky and sets a few hours before sunrise.

A moonless evening, then a last-quarter moon. The Moon reaches last quarter today (20:28 UTC, 12:28 PM PST) and does not rise until around midnight, so the whole first evening of standard time — sunset 5:09 PM — is properly dark. Before dawn Monday it stands near Mars and Jupiter in Leo.

After midnight, the parade builds. Mars rises around 1:00 AM, Jupiter about 30 minutes behind it, the two drawing toward their close meeting on November 15–16. The Pleiades climb in the east through the evening, and the South Taurid meteors peak midweek (Nov 4–5) — few in number, but slow and bright.

Honest about our sky. The valley floor is bright — from town you will get the Moon, the planets, and the boldest stars, and that is a fair show. For the rest, it is 45 minutes up Mt. Hamilton Road toward Lick Observatory: at 4,200 feet the Milky Way has structure, the Pleiades become a cluster instead of a smudge, and Saturn’s rings in a small scope will stop a conversation.

Set each morning; carried forward at midday and evening. Sources: EarthSky — Visible planets tonight · The Weather Network — Mars and Jupiter in November
