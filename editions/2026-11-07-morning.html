📜 This Day in History

Saturday, November 7

1989 — A valley tremor in the Loma Prieta aftershock season. On November 7, 1989 — three weeks after the Loma Prieta earthquake — a magnitude 4.2 quake struck about 10 miles southwest of San Jose in the late afternoon. A modest tremor on its own; in a valley still clearing rubble and sleeping lightly, every shake that autumn was news. The local well for November 7 is thin beyond this item, so today’s box honestly widens to California and national entries below.

1872 — The University of California gets its second president. On November 7, 1872, Daniel Coit Gilman was inaugurated as the second president of the University of California. Gilman went on to shape the young university in its Oakland and Berkeley years before leaving to found Johns Hopkins — a California institution-building moment from the state’s first university decade.

1916 — Jeannette Rankin is elected to Congress. On November 7, 1916, Montana voters elected Jeannette Rankin to the U.S. House of Representatives — the first woman elected to either chamber of Congress, four years before the 19th Amendment gave women the vote nationwide.

1917 — The Bolshevik Revolution. On November 7, 1917 (October 25 by the old Russian calendar), Bolshevik forces led by Vladimir Lenin overthrew Russia’s provisional government in Petrograd — the October Revolution that reshaped the century.

1944 — Roosevelt wins a fourth term. On November 7, 1944, with the Second World War still raging, American voters returned Franklin D. Roosevelt to the White House for an unprecedented fourth term, defeating Thomas E. Dewey.

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Saturday, November 7 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up

Saturn is the evening planet — catch it early, before the clouds. Saturn is up at dark and high in the south, a steady golden naked-eye point, highest around 9:00 PM, with the Great Square of Pegasus overhead and the Pleiades rising in the east. The catch tonight: the NWS forecast turns mostly cloudy as the evening wears on (low near 51°F), so the first hour or two after the 5:03 PM sunset is the viewing window — the reverse of last night’s clear-all-night sky.

A moonless evening — the Moon is nearly gone. The waning crescent is down to about 2 percent lit and stays out of the evening sky entirely. The New Moon arrives tomorrow night, Sunday Nov 8 (about 11:00 PM PT) — tonight and tomorrow are the darkest evenings of the month, clouds permitting.

Taurids still drifting between the peaks. The Southern Taurids peaked overnight Nov 4–5 and remain active — only about 5–10 an hour even under ideal skies, but slow, bright, and prone to fireballs. The Northern Taurids follow, peaking Nov 11–12. Tonight’s building cloud is the obstacle, not the Moon.

Late night and before dawn: Mars and Jupiter rise. Reddish Mars and bright Jupiter come up late — around 11:30 PM and midnight — in the east, and brilliant Venus is the dawn star, low in the east-southeast before sunrise. Mercury remains lost in the Sun’s glare this week; do not go looking for it.

Honest about our sky. The valley floor is bright — from town, on a clear early evening like the one forecast tonight, you will get Saturn, the planets and the boldest stars, and a Taurid fireball can punch through city light. For fainter meteors and the rest, it is 45 minutes up Mt. Hamilton Road toward Lick Observatory: at 4,200 feet the Milky Way has structure and Saturn’s rings in a small scope will stop a conversation.

Set each morning; carried forward at midday and evening. Sources: StarWalk — Sky tonight · EarthSky — Visible planets tonight, November · Astronomy — The Sky This Week, Nov 6–13
