📜 This Day in History

Honestly: no San Jose item for November 19 was found in the standing local sources this morning — the SJPL timeline and History San José bench runs dry for this date, so the box says so and reaches wider, as the standing rule allows. Two local notes frame the date: San Jose’s founding anniversary is ten days away, on November 29, 1777, and the city’s weather records for November 19 stand at a high of 79°F in 1936 and a low of 28°F in 1893.

1863 — Lincoln delivers the Gettysburg Address. On November 19, 1863, President Abraham Lincoln spoke for about two minutes — some 272 words — at the dedication of the Soldiers’ National Cemetery at Gettysburg, four and a half months after the battle, following a two-hour oration by Edward Everett. The address framed the Civil War as a test of a nation conceived in liberty, and charged the living with the unfinished work.

1969 — Apollo 12 lands on the Moon, precisely. On November 19, 1969, the lunar module Intrepid, with Pete Conrad and Alan Bean, touched down in the Ocean of Storms — the second crewed Moon landing, targeted close to the earlier Surveyor 3 probe — while Richard Gordon remained in orbit.

1919 — the Senate rejects the Treaty of Versailles. On November 19, 1919, the U.S. Senate voted on competing versions of the World War I peace treaty and neither reached the two-thirds needed for ratification — a turning point that kept the United States out of the League of Nations.

1977 — Sadat goes to Israel. Egyptian President Anwar Sadat arrived in Israel on November 19, 1977, the first Arab leader to visit, ahead of an address to the Knesset about peace — a visit that led toward the Camp David Accords.
