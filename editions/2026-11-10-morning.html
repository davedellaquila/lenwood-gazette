📜 This Day in History

Tuesday, November 10

1988 — A magnitude 4.8 earthquake east of San Jose. On November 10, 1988 (UTC), a moderate magnitude 4.8 quake struck about 9 miles east of San Jose at a shallow depth of about 5 miles, rated light (intensity IV) in the city, with a string of small aftershocks in the hours that followed. It is the clearest local entry for this date in the catalogs checked this morning — the local well for November 10 is honestly thin beyond it, so the box widens below, as it says when it does.

1957 — A record football crowd in San Francisco. On November 10, 1957, 102,368 people attended the San Francisco 49ers–Los Angeles Rams game — the largest regular-season crowd in NFL history at the time, and still a Bay Area sporting landmark.

1775 — The Marine Corps is founded. On November 10, 1775, the Second Continental Congress authorized two battalions of Continental Marines — the founding the U.S. Marine Corps still celebrates as its birthday, on the eve of tomorrow's Veterans Day.

1969 — Sesame Street debuts. On November 10, 1969, Sesame Street premiered on National Educational Television (the forerunner of PBS), with Jim Henson's Muppets alongside their human neighbors — a first episode, hosted by Gordon showing a girl named Sally around the neighborhood, that changed children's television.

1975 — The Edmund Fitzgerald sinks. On November 10, 1975, the 729-foot freighter SS Edmund Fitzgerald sank in a storm on Lake Superior, killing all 29 crew members — the worst single accident in the lake's history, later memorialized in Gordon Lightfoot's song.

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Tuesday, November 10 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up

Saturn again — and tonight the sky is clear for it. Saturn is the evening planet — up at dark, high in the south, a steady golden naked-eye point — with the Great Square of Pegasus overhead, Cassiopeia in the northeast, and the Pleiades climbing in the east. Tonight's forecast is mostly clear near 53°F after the 5:00 PM sunset, so unlike Monday's early cloud, the clean window starts right at dusk.

The young Moon returns — barely: a thin crescent near Antares, low in the southwest. The Moon, new on Sunday night, is about two days old tonight and only about 4 percent lit, sitting close to bright red Antares low above the western horizon after sunset and setting about 30 minutes after the Sun. From the valley floor it is a real challenge — binoculars and a clear southwest horizon, 20 to 30 minutes after sunset — and it is gone long before it can spoil the dark. Tomorrow evening's crescent will be easier.

Taurids peak tomorrow night — tonight is the last rehearsal. Both Taurid branches are active — only a few meteors an hour, but slow, bright, and prone to fireballs — and the Northern Taurids peak on the night of Nov 11–12, with the thin crescent setting early and leaving the night dark. Any bright slow streak tonight is likely a Taurid warming up.

Before dawn Wednesday: Venus tight by Spica, and Mars closing on Jupiter. Brilliant Venus shines in the east-southeast an hour before sunrise, about 1.2 degrees from Spica, Virgo's brightest star. Higher up, Mars sits about 2.4 degrees from Jupiter in Leo, closing toward their striking meeting on Nov 16; Mercury is also back in the morning sky but still faint in the twilight this week.

Honest about our sky. The valley floor is bright — from town you will get Saturn, the boldest stars, and any Taurid fireball, which can punch through city light; the crescent near Antares is the one item on tonight's list that city light and haze may simply take. For the full benefit of these dark nights, it is 45 minutes up Mt. Hamilton Road toward Lick Observatory: at 4,200 feet the Milky Way has structure and Saturn's rings in a small scope will stop a conversation.
