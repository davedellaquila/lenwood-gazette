📜 This Day in History

Wednesday, November 11

1918 — The tradition behind today's parade begins. San Jose's Veterans Day Parade has been produced every year since 1918, with city and county support — which is why today's downtown march is the 108th, and billed as the largest Veterans Day parade in Northern California. It grew directly out of the armistice celebrations below.

1918 — The armistice: the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month. Fighting in World War I stopped at 11:00 AM on November 11, 1918, six hours after Germany signed the armistice in a railway carriage at Compiègne. President Wilson proclaimed the first Armistice Day a year later; in 1954 the holiday was renamed Veterans Day to honor veterans of all wars — the day San Jose marks today.

1921 — The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier is dedicated at Arlington. On November 11, 1921, exactly three years after the armistice, an unidentified American soldier from World War I was interred at Arlington National Cemetery in a state funeral officiated by President Warren G. Harding — the tomb now guarded around the clock and inscribed to a soldier "known but to God."

1904 / 1911 / 1983 — San Jose's November 11 weather marks. The date's local record book is its honest local entry beyond the parade: the hottest November 11 in San Jose reached 82°F in 1904, the coldest fell to 31°F in 1911, and the wettest brought 1.40 inches of rain in 1983. Today's sunny 79°F comes close to — but does not threaten — the 1904 mark.

⭐ Star Chart — tonight over the valley

Tonight · Wednesday, November 11 · chart for ~7:00 PM, looking up

Saturn in a mostly clear sky — and the crescent is out of the way early. Saturn is again the evening planet — up at dark, high in the south, a steady golden point — with the Great Square of Pegasus overhead, Cassiopeia in the northeast, and the Pleiades climbing in the east. Tonight's forecast holds mostly clear — confirmed at evening press time — low near 53°F after the 5:00 PM sunset. The Moon is a thin waxing crescent, only a few percent lit, and sets early — by full dark it is gone, leaving the night properly dark.

Tonight is the peak: the Northern Taurids, Nov 11–12. This is the shower's peak night. Expect only about five meteors an hour at best — a quiet shower — but Taurids are slow, bright, and prone to fireballs that can punch through city light. Best after midnight, when Taurus is high; any bright, slow streak tonight is very likely a Taurid. The early-setting crescent means moonlight will not spoil it.

Before dawn Thursday: Venus brilliant, and Mars closing on Jupiter. Venus blazes at about magnitude −4.7 in the east-southeast before sunrise. Higher in Leo, Mars and Jupiter are closing toward their close meeting on the mornings of Nov 15–16, when they pass about a degree apart near Regulus — worth watching night by night as the gap shrinks. The Leonids follow, peaking Nov 16–17.

Honest about our sky. From the valley floor tonight you will get Saturn, the boldest stars, and any Taurid fireball — the shower's faint, ordinary meteors will mostly be lost to city light, and that is the honest trade. For the full peak, it is 45 minutes up Mt. Hamilton Road toward Lick Observatory: at 4,200 feet, under this moonless-after-dusk sky, the Taurids' slow trains and the Milky Way's structure both open up.
